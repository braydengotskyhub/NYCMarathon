Thank you

My parents and I after the race

Getting to the start line took some work. Over the spring and summer I reached out to more than 50 charities and ended up on waitlist after waitlist. Then, 45 days before the race, Every Mother Counts offered me a spot and gave me 48 hours to decide whether I could train and fundraise in time.

My mom and sister told me I'd regret it if I passed, so I said yes. I'm so glad I did, and you made the rest possible. Thank you for being part of it.

All of it went to Every Mother Counts, which works to make pregnancy and childbirth safe for every mother, everywhere.

MILE 0  /  STATEN ISLAND

Before the start

Last year, I cheered on my sister at the Chicago Marathon, then cheered on strangers at the New York City Marathon. Standing on the sidelines, you see the best of people, and this year I wanted to see it from the runner's side.

I ran for Every Mother Counts because my mom has supported me my whole life, and everyone deserves a person like that. True to form, she went all in: shirts and totes for everyone cheering us on, bracelets for me and my roommate to run in, and iron-on letters for my race shirt so strangers could yell my name, which has always been my favorite thing to do as a spectator.

My longest training run was 20 miles, three weeks out. I started in Brooklyn trying to beat the rain (I didn't), ran the length of Manhattan, looped Central Park and crossed the Queensboro Bridge. It went faster than I expected, and that's when I knew I could do this.

MILES 1–26  /  FIVE BOROUGHS

Out on the course

The start on the Verrazzano Bridge had no crowds at all, just runners, which is why Brooklyn hit even harder: crazy crowds, bands, signs, all of it. My favorite thing was giving high fives to everyone and interacting with every sign and person I could, and that energy is what kept me going. I spotted my family for the first time at mile 7.

The stretch I'd anticipated most was the Queensboro Bridge at mile 16. It's the steepest climb on the course and completely silent. Then you come off it into Manhattan and the loudest crowd of the day is waiting at the bottom. I had an instant smile on my face, and my friends and family were right there after the bridge.

The hardest part came around mile 20, through the Bronx and Harlem: two more bridges, thinner crowds, and that's when the pain really sets in. I could feel a cramp building in my left calf (it never fully hit), and the Gatorade volunteers probably hated to see me coming as I pumped every electrolyte I could find. What kept me going was knowing Central Park was close, with my family waiting at mile 24 and friends at mile 25. Fifth Avenue is a long uphill and it gets harder the longer you're on it, but the crowds got bigger the whole way, and that's what made it doable.

MILE 26.2  /  CENTRAL PARK

The finish

Finish time
3:45
Pace
8:38/mile

Crossing the line, the first thing I felt was gratitude for everyone who showed up, whether family, friends or complete strangers, and for the chance to do something like this at all. About a minute later the pain set in, and the event staff kept the herd of finishers moving forward, even though every one of our bodies wanted to stop.

Every time I saw my family and friends, my pace picked up. You can see it in my splits. I stopped three times to hug family and friends, and each time my mom sent me right back out. She didn't want the hugs slowing me down, because she wanted me to hit my goal time even more than I did. I finished within a minute of it. She was right, naturally.

What I'll remember most is the support, and how many different forms it took. There were the donations that kept climbing, the texts and messages that landed all week, and the friends and family who showed up in person, some of them in the shirts and totes my mom made. There were my sister and my roommate, who knew exactly what this took, and the strangers who yelled my name off my shirt and gave me a high five just when I needed one. Your generosity blew me away. It carried me past my own goal and helped Every Mother Counts surpass its NYC fundraising goal too. I appreciate every one of you.

BONUS ROUND

FAQ

Would you do it again?

Yes. Easily. But first I want to run one of the other World Marathon Majors. Hint hint.

Did you stop at all?

Three times, to hug family and friends. My mom cut the hugs short by telling me to keep running.

What was your favorite part?

The Queensboro Bridge. Total silence on the climb, then the loudest and most saturated crowd of the day waiting at the bottom. Instant smile.

What was the hardest part?

Miles 20 to 23. Two bridges, thin crowds, a left calf threatening to cramp, and then Fifth Avenue decided to be uphill.

How much Gatorade did you drink?

Enough that the volunteers hated to see me coming.

Best thing you saw on the course?

The Kool-Aid Man. OH YEAH.

What was on your training playlist?

The Wicked soundtrack.
